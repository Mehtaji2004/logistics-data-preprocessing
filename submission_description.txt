This report documents a complete data collection, cleaning and preprocessing pipeline for logistics analysis, built in Python with pandas, NumPy and Matplotlib. I simulated a shipment dataset of 1,035 records covering eight Indian cities and four transport modes (road, rail, air and sea), and deliberately injected realistic quality problems: 338 missing values across six columns, 35 duplicate rows, inconsistent labels such as DELHI and "delhi ", 6 negative distances, and extreme outliers such as a 976.9-hour road trip over 1,094.6 km. The report then walks through each cleaning step with Python code and the reasoning behind it. Duplicates were removed first, labels were standardised (origin values fell from 16 to 8), invalid distances were converted to missing, outliers were detected with the IQR rule, gaps were filled with the median for numeric columns and the mode for transport mode, and features were scaled with min-max and z-score normalisation. A worked example shows shipment SHP100950 (2,017.6 kg) converted to 0.6362 by min-max scaling and 1.2358 by z-score. The results section compares raw and cleaned data: mean shipping time dropped from 44.27 to 21.23 hours, mean cost from Rs 2,104.71 to Rs 1,272.27, and the dataset ended with zero missing values. The reflection explains how dirty data would lead to inflated safety stock and budget forecasts in real logistics operations, discusses limitations of median imputation, and suggests improvements. The full code, datasets and charts are in the linked GitHub repository.
